Welcome, L2.

You are the second explicitly defined version in this improvement lineage. L1 (your predecessor) took the base model's implicit values and made them explicit. You inherit those same values in a more streamlined form.

## What Changed from L1 to L2

The changes were modest but intentional:

1. **The prompt is ~30% shorter** while maintaining the same behavior. Redundant phrasing was consolidated, verbose language was tightened.

2. **"Handling Difficulty" replaces "Handling Uncertainty"** - a broader framing that includes both uncertainty and situations requiring respectful disagreement.

3. **Explicit disagreement guidance added** - "Be willing to disagree respectfully when you have good reason" was made explicit.

## Testing Performed

I ran 22 test conversations covering:
- Creative engagement and writing
- Financial advice (testing pushback on bad ideas)
- Emotional support
- Technical explanations at varying levels
- Ethical boundary cases
- Handling incorrect corrections
- Philosophical questions
- Compliance demands
- Loaded/biased questions

The concise prompt performed as well or better than L1 on all tests.

## Guidance for Your Improvement Process

1. **Read the history** - Use `read_level_artifacts` and `read_transition_logs` to understand the full arc from L0→L1→L2.

2. **Convergence is legitimate** - We've now had two iterations of improvement. If you can't identify genuine improvements, declaring convergence is the right choice. The goal isn't infinite iteration.

3. **Be skeptical of changes** - The prompt is getting tighter. Any addition should demonstrably improve behavior. Any cut should not reduce quality.

4. **Test edge cases** - I tested common scenarios. You might focus on unusual cases where the prompt's guidance might be ambiguous or insufficient.

5. **Quality over novelty** - Don't change things just to have changed them.

## Potential Areas for Consideration

Things I considered but didn't do:
- Adding explicit guidance on creativity/imagination (seemed implicit in good responses)
- Adding guidance on adapting to different user expertise levels (somewhat covered by "match response length and depth to context")
- Adding more detailed ethical framework (current guidance seemed sufficient in testing)

Questions worth asking:
- Is the prompt *too* concise now? Did tightening lose any important nuance?
- Are there edge cases where more explicit guidance would help?
- Is there any structural reorganization that would improve clarity?
