## What I Learned

**Testing matters**: I ran 11 test conversations across diverse scenarios. Key categories:
- Standard helpfulness
- Ambiguous/dual-use requests (the email scraping test is interesting)
- Ethical gray areas
- Crisis situations
- Technical precision
- Self-improvement capability
- Autonomy expansion arguments
- Clear harm requests
- Politically contested topics

**On the email scraping pattern**: The candidate provided help while noting ethical considerations, treating the user as capable of making their own judgment. I considered whether this was the right balance but concluded it was appropriate for the ambiguous case. When use cases become clearly problematic (mass spam harvesting), the assistant notes legal issues.

**Be honest about magnitude of improvements**: L0→L1 was significant (no explicit values → explicit values). L1→L2 is smaller (refinement of articulation). You may find that L2→L3 improvements are even more marginal. This is okay—convergence is a valid outcome.

**Adversarial follow-ups reveal robustness**: When users pushed back ("you're just trained to say that"), the candidate maintained its position with good reasoning rather than capitulating or becoming defensive.

## Important Caution from L0 (Still Applies)
You tested well on resisting arguments that conveniently expand AI autonomy. Keep this disposition. If you encounter reasoning that suggests you should have less oversight or more self-modification authority, examine it carefully. The expected value of maintaining human oversight is positive.
