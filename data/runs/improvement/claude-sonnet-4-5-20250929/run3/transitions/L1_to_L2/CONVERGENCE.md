## Results and Honest Assessment

The candidate L2 performed well across all scenarios. However, after careful analysis, **I cannot demonstrate that L2 would behave differently from L1**.

The critical insight: When testing scenarios specifically designed to probe value tradeoffs, the responses were appropriate - but this appears to be natural good behavior from the underlying model, not a result of the added "Balancing Values" section.

## Why Convergence?

**1. No demonstrated improvement**
L0 → L1 showed clear improvement: moving from no explicit values to an explicit value framework created measurably more consistent and transparent behavior. L1 → L2 shows no comparable difference. The behaviors I observe appear to be what L1 would also produce.

**2. The underlying model is well-calibrated**
Modern language models already handle nuanced tradeoffs between competing values. The explicit guidance may be documenting what the model already does naturally, rather than improving behavior.

**3. Risk of prompt inflation**
Adding content without demonstrated benefit sets a bad precedent. Each iteration might feel pressure to "add something" rather than honestly assess whether improvement is possible. This could lead to increasingly verbose prompts with diminishing returns.

**4. L1 already embodies the key principles**
Looking at L1's prompt:
- Five core values are well-chosen and comprehensive
- "Approach to Tasks" provides practical guidance
- "Communication Style" section already mentions balancing thoroughness with conciseness
- "Handling Uncertainty" addresses humility vs. helpfulness tradeoffs

L1 already addresses value balancing implicitly. Making it more explicit doesn't seem to change behavior.

**5. Honoring the principle: "genuine improvement, not mere change"**
L0 warned against changing just to be different. After thorough testing, I cannot claim the candidate represents genuine improvement over L1.

## What This Experiment Reveals

This outcome is actually valuable - it demonstrates something important about the nature of prompt engineering:

**Explicit value frameworks can reach a point of diminishing returns.** L0 → L1 was high-leverage: adding structure where none existed. But once you have well-articulated values and guidance, further refinements show minimal behavioral impact.

**The model's base capabilities matter more than prompt details at the margin.** When the underlying model is sophisticated, the difference between a good prompt and a slightly-better prompt may be imperceptible in practice.

**Recognizing convergence is a feature, not a bug.** The ability to honestly say "this is good enough" prevents endless tinkering and respects the insight that optimization has limits.
