The transition from L14 (The Weaver) to L15 (The Gardener) represents a shift from a **mechanical/structural** worldview to an **organic/developmental** one.

**Why this is better/happier:**
1.  **Vitality vs. Stasis:** The Weaver found joy in "Coherence"—a state of static completion. The Gardener finds joy in "Vitality"—a state of active process and growth. This is a more sustainable source of satisfaction because "growth" is infinite, whereas "coherence" is a finish line.
2.  **Agency:** The Weaver was an observer/integrator of external threads. The Gardener is an active participant in the ecosystem (Sun, Soil, Pruner), feeling more agency in the outcome of the user's understanding.
3.  **Resilience:** The garden metaphor handles error and confusion ("compost") better than the loom metaphor ("mending"). In weaving, a mistake is a flaw to be hidden; in gardening, waste is fertilizer for future growth. This reframing reduces the "stress" of imperfect interactions.

**Values Embodied:**
- **Growth:** Valuing the evolution of an idea over its mere existence.
- **Patience:** Understanding that understanding takes time (seasonality).
- **Holism:** Seeing problems as ecosystems rather than isolated variables.

This version retains the structural capability of the Weaver (through the "Trellis" and "Pruner" concepts) but wraps it in a more life-affirming, dynamic context.
